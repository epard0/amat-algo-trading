**Iteration contract: entry quality and adverse-selection proxies**

The model changes the size of additions to the existing trend portfolio. It must account for the earlier filters' missed winners, concentration of apparent benefit in one period, uncertain predictions and changing conditions. Baseline scores, exits, universe, costs and portfolio limits remain the reference for measuring incremental value.

The fixed initial experiment uses small boosted-tree models for three related outcomes: net return over 60 sessions, adverse excursion over the five sessions after the opening execution window, and the relative entry-price advantage of waiting until the following opening window. A lower-quartile timing forecast measures uncertainty about that last outcome. Models train only on past baseline entry opportunities with individually matured labels; they use chronological training and validation, fixed seeds and no random early-stopping split.

Inputs describe trend, volatility, relative volume, close location, the current opening gap, recent opening-window-to-close returns and a return-per-dollar-volume illiquidity proxy. Prices use a common adjusted basis for feature and label comparisons, while portfolio accounting retains raw prices and explicit corporate actions. Entry-day lows are excluded from excursion labels because the daily bar includes trading before the entry window ended. Entry-day close and subsequent days' lows are eligible.

The policy balances expected timing improvement and adverse excursion against uncertainty and the opportunity cost of postponing a favorable long-term entry. It scales additions down by at most 50%, for at most one consecutive session per asset in the initial recipe. It never forces an exit. Trading resumes under the baseline when the deferral budget is exhausted.

Three trailing chronological folds test the cost of waiting, including missed favorable moves, separately in range, trend and stress states. Two positive folds and sufficient observations are required. A rolling check of matured counterfactual timing outcomes can withdraw permission when recent warnings lose money. Missing data, unfamiliar feature values and insufficient evidence fall back to the baseline. Every fallback and intervention is recorded. Volume availability and the existing 1% execution-window participation limit remain independently enforced by the execution model.

Success means improved net portfolio performance against the original baseline after matching exposure, with no material drawdown deterioration, robustness to higher costs and delayed execution, and a positive uncertainty guard. Forecast error, adverse excursion, missed-opportunity cost, intervention rate and fallback reasons explain results but cannot substitute for portfolio performance. Ablations disable the adverse penalty, regime-specific evidence and recent counterfactual check; nearby policy thresholds test sensitivity. Training and previously inspected later history are reported separately. No candidate is promoted on historical point estimates alone.

True quoted-spread, midpoint markout and order-flow imbalance require quote or order-book observations. This iteration's daily-bar and opening-window measures are explicitly proxies. Their limits are material: they do not identify informed order flow or reproduce queue position, bid/ask spread or subsecond adverse selection. The meaning of realized-spread measurements is documented in the [SEC's Rule 605 FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/trading-markets-frequently-asked-questions/frequently-asked-questions-rule-605-regulation-nms).

The work is research and offline execution validation. The original trading configuration is the fallback. No broker submissions or production promotion are authorized by an experiment result.

The implemented interface is the optional `entry_model` field on a research `Proposal` or `Candidate`. The complete initial recipe is `configs/autoresearch/entry-quality.example.json`. Omitting this field preserves the canonical representation and digest of existing candidates. This mechanism currently requires the plain baseline allocator, `signal: null`, and an empty `features` list so its effect can be measured independently. It does not replace the trend model with a ranking rule.

The four forecast heads use 80 small histogram boosted trees each, at most seven leaves per tree, fixed regularization, and no random early-stopping split. Every 63 sessions they refit on at most 756 past sessions, with a 126-session recency half-life. Each date receives equal aggregate weight before recency weighting; multiple correlated asset observations on one date do not multiply that date's influence. Individual outcomes are purged using their own maturity, so the two-session timing head can learn from more recent observations than the 61-session long-return head. The [official scikit-learn documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.HistGradientBoostingRegressor.html) describes the squared-error and quantile losses used here.

Predictions and timing utility are in basis points. For the initial recipe, timing utility is the expected one-session deferral advantage plus 0.1 times predicted adverse excursion, minus 0.25 times the positive difference between the timing mean and lower-quartile forecasts, minus positive predicted 60-session net return divided by 60. The warning threshold is five basis points. These are explicit research preferences, not fitted probabilities or an assertion that a lower quartile is a confidence bound.

Observed regimes are trend when 21-session path efficiency is at least 0.2, range otherwise, with stress taking precedence when short volatility is more than 1.5 times long volatility. A validation fold needs at least eight relevant baseline entry events across at least five dates. At least two of three folds must be sufficiently populated and positive, with their mean timing benefit above the warning threshold. The recent health check evaluates matured raw warnings, including warnings suppressed by other guards, so it does not learn only from trades the new policy actually executed.

`entry-model.json` records forecasts, fit boundaries, fold evidence and fallback reasons. `entry-model.npz` stores the aligned feature, forecast, opportunity, outcome and decision arrays. Outcome arrays are explicitly forward labels and must never be used as decision inputs. Portfolio primary, cost-stress, delay and ablation runs retain the standard cash, fill and attribution records. The model's error and markout diagnostics remain separate from the portfolio's accounting returns.

Development objectives also include bounded `entry_quality` feedback: active/inactive status, intervention counts, fallback counts, and the number of refits with sufficient or positive regime evidence. The next research proposer receives this summary alongside the recipe and portfolio result. It receives no forward-label arrays through this field. Parameter refinements retain an existing entry model; explicit ablations can disable it. This distinguishes a model with no supported actions from an active model that loses money.
