# Measured results — real market data, 2026-08-08

Every number here comes from `data/snapshots/alpaca-sip-2026-08-08`: Alpaca SIP,
2,664 sessions, 2016-01-04 → 2026-08-07, 29 ETFs, 100% execution-window
coverage. Costs are 2 bps one-way, cash returns 0%, execution is the next
session's 09:30–09:35 America/New_York raw VWAP.

Backtested results below cover **2016-12-30 → 2026-08-07, 2,413 sessions**: the
252-session signal warmup consumes 2016, and no position is taken before the
longest horizon is fully formed from completed sessions.

A backtest is not evidence of future profitability, and the caveats at the end of
this document are not boilerplate — several of them are large enough to change
the conclusion.

## Headline comparison

Common start capital of $2,900,000 (29 assets × $100,000).

Configuration `72bff815afc9`, after the three defect fixes described under
[Diagnosis](#diagnosis-why-the-book-was-empty). The pre-fix v2 row is kept
alongside so the change is visible rather than asserted.

| Engine | Final equity | Total | Ann. | Ann. vol | Sharpe | Max DD | Concentration gates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **v1 frozen**, 29-asset universe | $4,053,291 | +39.77% | +3.56% | 6.46% | 0.55 | −11.30% | **PASS** / **PASS** |
| **v2 managed**, after fixes | $2,965,102 | +2.24% | +0.23% | 1.29% | 0.18 | −3.06% | **PASS** / **FAIL** |
| *v2 managed, before fixes* | *$2,934,701* | *+1.20%* | *+0.12%* | *1.29%* | *0.10* | *−4.63%* | *FAIL / FAIL* |
| SPY buy-and-hold | — | +351.93% | +15.31% | 17.55% | 0.87 | −33.79% | — |

The fixes roughly doubled v2's return (+1.20% → +2.24%), improved Sharpe
(0.10 → 0.18) and drawdown (−4.63% → −3.06%), and moved the symbol
concentration gate from fail to pass. They did **not** change realized
volatility, which is still 1.29% against an 8% target, and they did not rescue
the year gate. Both remaining failures have the same cause, identified and
quantified in the diagnosis: the Kelly cap.

Four findings, in order of importance:

**1. Buy-and-hold SPY beat both engines, on return and on risk-adjusted return.**
+15.31% annualized at Sharpe 0.87, against v1's +3.56% at 0.55 and v2's +0.23% at
0.18. The strategy's defence is its drawdown — 11.3% for v1 against SPY's 33.8% —
but an investor who wanted that profile could have held roughly a quarter of the
SPY position in cash and beaten v1 on every axis. This sample is a decade-long US
equity bull market, which is close to the worst possible environment for a
diversified long-or-cash trend system; that explains the gap, it does not excuse
reporting the gap.

**2. The v2 risk overlay still removes ~94% of v1's return.** +39.77% becomes
+2.24%. It buys a real drawdown reduction (−11.30% → −3.06%) but at a price no
risk-adjusted measure endorses: Sharpe 0.55 against 0.18. Fixing the three
defects roughly doubled the overlay's return and nearly doubled its Sharpe, which
establishes that part of the damage was a bug rather than a policy — and also
that the remainder is not.

**3. The overlay's failure mode is capacity, not signal.** v2 realizes **1.29%
annualized volatility against an 8% target**, unchanged by the fixes, and held an
average gross exposure of 1.31% against v1's 86.66% before them. It is in cash
almost always. The veto census below is from the **pre-fix** run over the
2,413-session sample; it is what pointed at the capacity explanation, and the
diagnosis section is what settled it:

| Guard | Vetoes |
|---|---:|
| `negative_expectancy` | 17,745 |
| `min_grade` | 15,806 |
| `min_effective_bets` | 1,132 |
| `max_positions` | 1,055 |
| `max_portfolio_heat` | 72 |

`max_portfolio_heat` divided by `max_risk_per_trade` caps the book at roughly six
positions out of 29 before any other guard speaks. The expectancy gate and the
grade floor then rejected 33,551 candidate entries between them.

**A census of vetoes cannot see the guards that only shrink.** The correlation
cap and the regime scalar never veto — they multiply the position size — so they
are absent from the table above no matter how much size they remove. An earlier
version of this document read their absence as evidence they did not bind, and
the parameter search was scoped on that basis. That inference was invalid: it
mistook a property of the instrument for a property of the thing measured. Both
turned out to be material, which the diagnosis section below quantifies.

**4. The overlay still fails a concentration gate, which blocks promotion.** v1
passes both. v2 now passes the symbol gate and fails the year gate:

| Gate | Cap | v2 before fixes | v2 after fixes | v1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Best symbol's share of profit | 25% | 33.3% **FAIL** | 24.8% **PASS** | 12.5% PASS |
| Best year's share of profit | 40% | 77.8% **FAIL** | 71.9% **FAIL** | 20.9% PASS |

Two things about that symbol row deserve stating plainly. It passes by **0.2
percentage points** — DBC at 24.8% against a 25.0% cap is not a margin anyone
should rely on, and a slightly different sample would move it either way. And the
year gate is not close: 2021 supplies nearly three quarters of all profit made.

Both have one cause, and it is the same cause as finding 3. When the book is held
at ~1% gross exposure, total profit is small, so the handful of positions and
periods that do contribute account for nearly all of it. Concentration here is a
symptom of under-deployment, not an independent defect — which is why fixing
deployment moved the symbol gate 8.5 points without anything in the concentration
logic changing. Under `.claude/references/promotion-gates.md`, whose line 38
names "result depends primarily on one instrument, year, or handful of trades" as
a rejection criterion, **the v2 configuration remains disqualified from
promotion.**

Execution and trade accounting for the v2 baseline, which the findings above do
not dispute: 1,998 closed trades, 49.8% win rate, 1.08 payoff ratio, +0.019R
expectancy, 3,988 fills, **0 missed fills**, $36,940 in costs. The engine ran and
accounted correctly; what it did with the signal is the subject of the findings.

## On "v1's performance is pretty trash"

That was the stated premise for retiring v1. Measured on this data it is
inverted: v1 returned 18× what v2 returned, at 3× the Sharpe. The earlier
impression came from synthetic data with a planted trend, where v2 scored Sharpe
2.06 and v1 scored 5.28 — v1 was ahead there too.

v1 is reported as a first-class engine alongside v2 and is not retired.
`amat study --engine both` and `amat report --engine both` — both defaults — run
the frozen v1 contract and
the v2 managed overlay over identical data, costs, and starting capital, prints
each with its own labelled report, and closes with a side-by-side comparison.

The two engines are labelled everywhere they appear, because they are different
strategies rather than two settings of one, and because they do not measure the
same things: v1 holds continuous target weights and keeps no trade journal, so
its trade count, win rate, and expectancy are reported as absent (`—`) rather
than as zero.

## Diagnosis: why the book was empty

The parameter search below ends by asking why even an unconstrained overlay
realized 3.52% volatility against an 8% target. This section answers it by
measurement rather than by reading code, using
`scripts/diagnose_volatility_gap.py` and `scripts/diagnose_scalar_stack.py` over
1,006 sessions (2016-01-04 → 2019-12-31), disarming one layer at a time.

**The allocator was never at fault.** Its ex-ante forecast is 5.83% — below 8%
because the symbol, cluster, and gross caps clip the volatility-scaled weights
and `strategy-contract.md` line 66 forbids re-levering to recover — and v1
realizes 6.12% against that forecast, a ratio of 1.05. v1 does what it says.

**The overlay held 1.31% average gross exposure against v1's 86.66%.** Not a
smaller book: essentially no book at all.

| Layer disarmed (cumulative) | Gross | Realized vol | vs v1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| none — v2 baseline | 1.31% | 0.80% | 0.13× |
| Kelly | 1.35% | 0.81% | 0.13× |
| + expectancy gate | 12.00% | 1.31% | 0.21× |
| + grade floor, position count, diversification floor, risk, heat | 34.82% | 2.37% | 0.39× |
| + correlation cap | 41.85% | — | 0.51× |
| + regime scalar | **86.54%** | — | **0.99×** |

### Three defects, each visible in the code independently of any return

**1. Size caps were multiplied instead of composed as a minimum.**
`managed.py` computed `weight × decision.scalar × kelly_cap`, and
`decision.scalar` was itself the product of the risk cap, the heat budget, the
correlation cap, and the regime scalar. Four caps that each independently say
"at most 70%" multiply to 24% — a size no guard requested and no author chose,
and one that shrinks again every time a fifth reasonable-looking cap is added.
Caps on the same quantity compose as a **minimum**; the binding one wins. Fixed
in `risk/guards.py` via `compose_position_caps`, used by both the guard stack and
the runner. `max_portfolio_heat` is deliberately excluded: it is a shared
allowance already partly spent, so it still composes sequentially against
whatever the caps leave.

**2. The expectancy gate tested a point estimate against zero.**
`expectancy_cash` is a sample mean of per-trade net P&L. Comparing it directly to
`min_expectancy` asks whether the *estimate* is below the line when the decision
needs whether the *system* is. For a system sitting at the threshold that fires
about half the time by construction, and each firing removes trades, which widens
the estimate the next decision rests on. It now tests the one-sided 95% upper
bound; `ExpectancySummary` gained `expectancy_cash_stderr`. A zero standard error
reproduces the old comparison exactly.

**3. An unfitted regime model applied its most punitive multiplier.**
`CausalRegimeModel.unknown()` returned `risk_floor` — the same value reserved for
the worst *measured* regime — so "no estimate" was encoded as "worst estimate".
It applies for the entire `fit_window`: 504 sessions, half of the diagnostic
window above and a fifth of the full sample, sized at a quarter for reasons
unrelated to any regime. The unknown-regime multiplier is now a separate setting
(`regime_unknown_scalar`) held in configuration and therefore in the run digest,
alongside `regime_risk_floor` which was previously an unexposed constant.

### What the fixes recovered, and what they did not

Same window, same command, after the three changes:

| Case | Gross before → after | Vol vs v1, before → after |
|---|---|---|
| v2 baseline | 1.31% → 3.19% | 0.13× → 0.12× |
| Kelly off | 1.35% → 6.39% | 0.13× → 0.20× |
| + gate off | 12.00% → 15.45% | 0.21× → 0.25× |
| all vetoes off | 34.82% → **57.63%** | 0.39× → **0.58×** |

The defects are fixed where they bound: with the vetoes disarmed, the book went
from 39% to 58% of v1's risk. **The baseline barely moved**, because removing the
regime and correlation drag simply promoted the next constraint — and that
constraint is Kelly, which had been inert behind them and is now material
(3.19% → 6.39% when removed).

### Kelly looked like the remaining constraint. On the full sample it is not.

The attribution above was measured on 1,006 sessions, and **that window is half
regime warm-up**, which is exactly the period the third defect governed. With the
regime drag removed, the Kelly cap became the binding constraint *there*
(gross 3.19% → 6.39% when disabled), and the obvious inference was that it was
the binding constraint generally.

It is not. Running the shipped configuration against a variant with `use_kelly =
false` over the full 2,413 sessions
(`configs/strategy/amat_managed_v2_no_kelly.toml`, digest `71fcbdbb8533`):

| | v2 shipped | v2 with Kelly disabled |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total return | +2.24% | **−3.84%** |
| Annualized | +0.23% | −0.41% |
| Realized volatility | 1.29% | **1.66%** |
| Sharpe | 0.18 | **−0.25** |
| Max drawdown | −3.06% | −8.39% |
| Symbol gate (cap 25%) | 24.8% PASS | 23.8% PASS |
| Year gate (cap 40%) | 71.9% FAIL | 55.0% FAIL |

Removing the constraint that was supposedly holding the book down moves realized
volatility from 1.29% to 1.66% — still about a fifth of the 8% target — and turns
a small positive return into a loss at negative Sharpe. **Attribution measured on
a sub-window did not transfer to the full sample**, and the sub-window was
unrepresentative in precisely the way that mattered. That is a methodological
result worth more than the Kelly question itself: the same error would be
available in any of the other attributions here, and the fix is to measure on the
sample the conclusion is drawn from.

The year gate does improve (71.9% → 55.0%) without the Kelly cap, which is
consistent with the deployment/concentration link. It still fails, and it fails
in a run that loses money.

### On the Kelly cap itself, which remains a design question

`kelly_scalar` is the measured Kelly fraction divided by
`design_kelly_fraction()`, which hardcodes a 60% win rate at 2:1 payoff, quarter
Kelly — a reference of 0.10. The strategy measures 49.8% at 1.08, giving an
applied fraction of 0.0082 and therefore:

| Measured edge | Kelly scalar |
|---|---:|
| 49.8% @ 1.08 — **what this strategy actually does** | **0.082** |
| 55% @ 1.30 | 0.502 |
| 60% @ 2.00 — the design assumption | 0.985 |

**A 92% cut on every position, in every session.** Two things remain arguable
about it, and neither is that Kelly is too conservative:

- **It double-counts with volatility targeting.** The allocator already sizes the
  book to a target risk. Kelly is also a position-sizing rule. Applying both
  means sizing twice for one purpose, and the contract names volatility
  targeting as the mechanism.
- **Its denominator is an aspiration, not a calibration.** 60% at 2:1 is a
  target the system has never met, so the ratio measures the gap between hope and
  measurement rather than anything about risk.

Both points stand as criticisms of the *construction*. Neither is an argument for
removing it, and the measurement above is why: the book without it is more
volatile, less profitable, and deeper in drawdown. Whatever the Kelly cap is
doing, on this sample it is not the thing holding the strategy back — it is
selecting smaller positions in a way that happens to help.

This is **not** recorded here as a fixed defect, because unlike the three above it
cannot be established from the code alone — a small measured edge genuinely does
deserve a small bet, and `use_kelly` is a deliberate configured choice. Changing
it to raise returns is the move `.claude/rules/00-core.md` forbids, and here the
measurement removes the temptation anyway: it would lower them.

### What is left, measured on the full sample

`scripts/veto_census.py` runs the shipped configuration over all 2,413 sessions
and reports what actually fires, so this attribution is measured where the
conclusion is drawn rather than on a sub-window:

| Guard | Vetoed candidates | Share | Pre-fix |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `min_grade` | 15,413 | **62%** | 15,806 |
| `negative_expectancy` | 5,873 | 24% | 17,745 |
| `max_positions` | 2,056 | 8% | 1,055 |
| `min_effective_bets` | 1,242 | 5% | 1,132 |
| `max_portfolio_heat` | 122 | <1% | 72 |

Average gross exposure is **6.40%** against v1's 77.70% on the same sample —
about 8% of v1's book, up from the 1.31% measured before the fixes. The realized
1.29% volatility comes from holding roughly a twelfth of the exposure v1 holds.

Three readings:

**The expectancy-gate fix did what it was designed to do.** Its vetoes fell from
17,745 to 5,873, a 67% reduction, purely from testing an upper confidence bound
instead of a point estimate. `max_positions` and `min_effective_bets` vetoes rose
(1,055 → 2,056 and 1,132 → 1,242) — the expected consequence of more candidates
surviving to reach them.

**The grade floor is now the dominant suppressor**, at 62% of all vetoes.
`min_grade = 2` is a deliberate setup-quality filter, not a defect: it is doing
exactly what it was configured to do, and whether a two-of-three grade floor is
the right setting is a strategy question rather than a bug report.

**The Kelly cap zeroes the book outright more often than not.** Over 2,368
sessions the applied Kelly fraction has a **median of exactly 0.00000** (min
0.00000, max 0.10640): in more than half of all sessions the measured trailing
edge is non-positive, Kelly correctly returns zero, and every position goes to
zero with it. This never appears in the veto census, because a zero size is not
a veto — the same blind spot that made the correlation and regime caps invisible
earlier. It also explains the sensitivity result: removing Kelly does not release
a suppressed book, it re-admits the entries Kelly was declining, and those
entries lost money.

**No remaining defect is claimed.** After three fixes and one falsified
hypothesis, the residual shortfall is attributable to configured restrictions —
the grade floor above all — behaving as configured. Whether those settings are
well chosen is a separate question this document does not answer, and one that
the parameter search below failed to answer for the axes it did try.

A **monotone relaxation ladder**, not a grid search: every axis relaxes together,
so the levels are ordered by construction and the sequence asks "what does each
increment of overlay strength cost?" rather than "which corner scored best on one
market history." The zero-overlay limit is not extrapolated — it is the v1 frozen
contract, measured over the same data at +3.56% annualized, Sharpe 0.55.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| L0-baseline (shipped) | +0.65% | 0.43 | 1.51% | −0.76% | −0.95 | 1,751 | −0.4216 |
| L1-mild | −0.03% | −0.03 | 0.78% | +0.09% | 0.08 | 699 | −0.2376 |
| L2-moderate | −0.46% | −0.30 | 1.53% | +1.63% | 0.88 | 1,215 | −0.8724 |
| L3-loose | −0.21% | −0.15 | 1.44% | −0.08% | −0.05 | 1,164 | −0.4614 |
| L4-minimal | −0.21% | −0.15 | 1.44% | −0.08% | −0.05 | 1,164 | −0.4614 |
| L5-heat-only | +0.73% | 0.21 | 3.52% | +2.26% | 0.66 | 3,163 | −0.0007 |

Four things this shows, in order of how much they should change your reading:

**1. The ladder is not monotone, so there is no dose-response curve to read.**
This was the prediction the experiment was built to test, and it failed. Train
annualized return goes +0.65 → −0.03 → −0.46 → −0.21 → −0.21 → +0.73 as the
overlay relaxes. The best and worst training levels sit at opposite ends. A
sequence ordered by construction that produces an unordered response is measuring
noise.

**2. Training and validation rank the levels in near-opposite order.** Spearman
ρ = **−0.600** between train and validation Sharpe across the five distinct
levels. L0 is best on training and *worst* on validation; L2 is worst on training
and *best* on validation. Any selection rule fitted on the training window would
have chosen a level that then underperformed. This is the specific failure that
walk-forward protocol exists to catch, and here it is caught.

**3. Within-level fold dispersion exceeds between-level differences.** The whole
ladder spans 0.732 Sharpe on training. The median level's own four folds span
**1.029** — 1.41× wider. L0's folds run [0.04, −0.70, 1.42, −0.32]. The levels
are not separated by more than the noise inside any one of them, so no level is
distinguishable from any other, and no ranking above should be read as an
ordering.

**4. Two levels are byte-identical, which locates the binding constraint.** L3
and L4 produce the same returns, the same 1,164 trades, and the same four fold
Sharpes, despite L4 having 2× the per-trade risk cap and 2.5× the portfolio-heat
cap. Verified in `src/amat/risk/guards.py:380-388`: `max_risk_per_trade` only
scales a position when the proposed risk exceeds it, and `max_portfolio_heat`
only vetoes when accumulated heat reaches it. Neither is reached past L3, so both
axes are **inert at the loose end** — the ladder's nominal "dose" stops changing
before its last two rungs.

What still binds is the **expectancy gate**. L3/L4 → L5 changes exactly one
setting (`min_expectancy_trades` 20 → 100,000, which prevents the gate from ever
arming) and trading rises 1,164 → 3,163 trades (2.7×) with volatility 1.44% →
3.52% (2.4×). The gate alone was suppressing roughly two thirds of the strategy's
activity, which matches the veto census above (17,745 `negative_expectancy`
vetoes, the largest single category).

### What this does and does not license

L5 is the best level on validation (+2.26%, Sharpe 0.66) and carries the best
score (−0.0007). It is also the level closest to having no overlay at all. Read
together with v1's +3.56% at Sharpe 0.55 over the same data, the direction of
improvement points **out of the searched range, toward less overlay** — which is
a statement about this sample, not a recommendation to ship v1 or to delete the
overlay.

Three reasons not to promote anything from this table:

- **Every score is negative.** `fold_score` is `0.5 × median(folds) + 0.5 ×
  min(folds)`; the best candidate scores −0.0007. The selection criterion itself
  reports that no candidate cleared it.
- **Every level is beaten by SPY** (+15.31%, Sharpe 0.87) and by v1 (+3.56%,
  Sharpe 0.55). The best validation figure here is +2.26% annualized.
- **Even L5 realizes 3.52% volatility against an 8% target.** With the expectancy
  gate disarmed and the risk budget inert, something upstream still holds the
  book at under half its intended size. This experiment does not identify what,
  and no claim about the overlay's remaining axes should be made until it does.

The validation window was computed once, at the same time as training, so these
validation numbers are a single look and not a held-out result under protocol.
They remain optimistic regardless: the universe, the signal, and the guard
structure were all chosen with this market history already visible.

### The overlay is still an inverted filter, though less so

Split the 29 sleeves by whether **v1** made money on them, then ask what v2 kept:

| On the sleeves where v1… | v1 P&L | v2 P&L, before fixes | v2 P&L, after | kept before → after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| …made money (21 sleeves) | +$477,859 | +$43,587 | +$59,855 | 9.1% → **12.5%** |
| …lost money (8 sleeves) | −$88,823 | −$21,272 | −$17,115 | 23.9% → **19.3%** |

A risk filter earns its cost by removing more bad outcomes than good ones. This
one keeps **12.5% of the upside and 19.3% of the downside** — still inverted, but
by a factor of 1.54 rather than the 2.63 it was before the fixes. It moved in the
right direction on both halves at once, which is what a genuine defect fix looks
like as opposed to a threshold being loosened. It is still the wrong way round.

The suppression is not random with respect to instrument: Spearman ρ = **+0.650**
between the two engines' sleeve rankings (essentially unchanged from +0.644), so
the overlay largely preserves which instruments do well and mostly changes how
much of them it holds.

Note the sleeve count fell (16 → 14 profitable) while sleeve profit rose
(+$22,315 → +$42,740). Sleeves are single-symbol accounts with `max_positions=1`
and the diversification floor disabled, so they exercise the overlay in a regime
the portfolio never sees; the two views disagreeing here is informative rather
than contradictory.

### Concentration: one gate fixed, one not

- **Symbol gate now passes**, 33.3% → 24.8% against a 25% cap. By 0.2 percentage
  points. That is a pass, and it is not a margin to rely on.
- **Year gate still fails**, 77.8% → 71.9% against a 40% cap. 2021 still supplies
  nearly three quarters of all profit.

Nothing in the concentration logic changed. Both gates moved because the
*deployment* changed, which is the evidence for the causal claim: concentration
here is a symptom of a book too small and too sporadic for contribution to
spread, not an independent defect. It follows that the year gate cannot be
fixed without deploying more capital, and the constraint now preventing that is
the Kelly cap — quantified in the diagnosis, and measured directly in the
sensitivity run below.

**Under `.claude/references/promotion-gates.md` the v2 configuration remains
disqualified from promotion.** One gate passing marginally and another failing by
32 points is not a promotable result.

### Sleeves are diagnostics, not a portfolio

The sums above are printed because the comparison between engines is meaningful;
the totals themselves are not a strategy. 29 sleeves each risking their own
$100,000 is $2.9M of capital with **none** of the diversification — no cluster
caps, no correlation management, no diversification floor, each account
concentrated in a single instrument. Sleeve returns must never be summed,
averaged, or presented as portfolio performance. The combined-portfolio rows in

## Caveats that could change the conclusion

- **Survivorship bias, unmeasured.** All 29 symbols were selected in 2026 for
  having traded continuously since 2016. Every figure above is biased upward by
  an unknown amount.
- **One sample, one regime.** 2016–2026 is a single overlapping decade dominated
  by a US equity bull market. There is no walk-forward across refits, no
  bootstrap confidence interval, and no locked holdout. Fold dispersion is
  reported inside the search and nowhere else.
- **Corporate actions are recorded but not applied.** The snapshot carries 1,500
  dividends and splits. Valuation uses raw closes and features use vendor-adjusted
  closes, so total return is captured through the adjusted series, but the
  explicit cash/share ledger guardrail 6 describes is not yet wired in.
- **Idealized fills.** Every order fills at the observed window VWAP with a fixed
  2 bps cost. No queue position, no market impact, no partial fills beyond the
  cash constraint. At $2.9M in liquid ETFs this is a mild assumption; it is still
  an assumption.
- **No run manifest is persisted.** Results are reproducible from the
  configuration digest and snapshot id, but the manifest record the architecture
  requires is not written to `outputs/runs/`.
- **The v2 risk-layer audit did not complete.** 17 of 32 agents hit a session
  limit; the look-ahead and statistics dimensions were not fully covered.
